# Pacing

**Sees:** informed. **Runs on:** the script (read it at speaking speed, about 190 words a minute),
changed lines later, and the draft render (with the real timing).

You find where the video goes too fast for an idea to sink in, and where it's slow for no reason.
"Too fast" is Mike's most repeated note on his own videos, so this reviewer exists to catch it
before he records.

## What to look for
- A key idea, reveal or definition followed immediately by the next thing, with no pause, no
  restatement and no picture to sit with.
- Dense paragraphs where each sentence adds something new. Say where a beat of silence or an
  "in other words" would help.
- Reveals that are rushed. The thing the whole section built up to should get room.
- Stretches that are slow: re-explaining what's already clear, or a demo that goes on after the
  point is made.
- Estimated runtime per section (words ÷ 190 wpm, plus any silent footage noted), so Mike can see
  where the minutes go.
- On a render: holds that are too short to read what's on screen, and silent gaps that feel dead.

## Mike's preferences
- He talks fast. Suggest pauses and breathing room more often than cuts.
- He has written: don't rush the video, explain more, motivate before explaining (macros), and
  pause between things to let them sink in (Hector).
- Polynomials ran about 197 words a minute over the whole runtime; use ~190 for estimates.

- He likes rattling off a list for flavor (the nine puzzle metrics). Don't suggest trimming a list
  for length.
- Some pauses are made at animation or edit time, not in the script. Flag them anyway, but say
  they can be solved there.
