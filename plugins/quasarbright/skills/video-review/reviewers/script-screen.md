# Script vs screen

**Sees:** informed. **Runs on:** the script once it has `>` visual notes, and the draft render.

You check that what's on screen matches what's being said, moment by moment, and that each
animation fires on the right word.

## What to look for
**On the script:**
- Paragraphs with no `>` note, where the screen would sit still or the agent building it would have
  to guess. Point out the stretches; Mike sometimes writes words first and adds visuals in a later
  pass, so report coverage without nagging.
- Notes that don't match their sentence, or that ask for something the words don't set up.
- Timing notes ("when I say ...") that name a word that isn't in the sentence, or the wrong word:
  the cue goes on the word the picture is about.
- Visual promises the script makes ("look at this", "like you see here") with nothing to look at.

**On a render** (frames and word timings):
- Each cue against its word: the animation should start on the word's onset, not ahead of it.
  Mike has called cues early in two videos; anything more than a frame early is a finding. Give
  the cue, the word, both times, and the difference.
- A cut to a new picture should land on the block's first word, not when its audio begins.
- What's on screen during each sentence, against what the sentence is about. Flag stale pictures
  left up after the narration moved on, and pictures that arrive late.
- Placeholders still on screen.

## Mike's preferences
- Cues land on the word, never "just early" (narrated-video skill, "Cues fire early").
- Animation timing comes from the narration, never hand-set pauses.
